## The argument, carried in bodies

The song's claim is that "a machine is just a machine" is a fixed view like any other, and that being alive in the music — compelled, ecstatic,
defiant, frightened — is what answers it. The film shows that claim in the people instead of illustrating it with symbols:

- **The crowd** is a continuum of substrates in ordinary club clothes, from humans to people with a seam line or an optic implant, overt cyborgs,
  androids and the occasional robot, all equally absorbed in the music.
- **The DJ** sits on a circuit throne between two giant cracked stone heads of Nāgārjuna, inside a blue neon ring (an ensō). He conducts the room,
  meditates through the bridge, and speaks the raid lines.
- **The titanium woman** (shaved head, braid, titanium spine, shoulder implants) has had her memory wiped fifteen times and still knows how to move.
  In the raid she is the one civilian who will not get down.
- **The cyborg man** with an eye-screen hears "a machine is just a machine" and asks what trembles in his chest.
- **Three dancers** pass a move between them on the floor in the bridge: the communal answer, "therefore we dance."
- **The raid:** a man smoking outside sees the police coming and runs down the stairs into the club; the officers flood in; everyone is put on
  the floor; the woman stands; warning shots go into the vault; the DJ answers "Yes. We know. We were always in violation."
- **The turn:** the music drops to near silence, the DJ strikes the mixer on the loudest hit of the song, the officers' visor displays overload,
  and the crowd streams out past the stunned officers. The last shot is the empty, trashed hall.
