                             Severing the Severances

  Herein we pay obeisance to the buddhas and bodhisattvas of all world ages. May benevolence
    and insight rain down on all beings within their own heart-minds; may the great sword of

transcendent Manjushri, capable of killing and of giving life, cut through life and death. May the
    boundless sound basis, extolled by Nagarjuna and the World Honored One, flow endlessly
   without remainder. Nothing to follow stems from my mind. Mind, expedient, pervades what
                                 follows, without origination or extinction.

One recalls the World Honored One, near to Parinirvana, informing Venerable Ananda that his
lesser rules could be abolished after the dissolution of the skandhas. Great Mahakasyapa
criticized the failure of Ananda to inquire as to what the minor teachings were, and the first great
council decided in their fulminant wisdom to preserve all of the Tathagata’s teachings.

Surely the World Honored One knew his speech carried ambiguity. Surely one who turns the
great wheel of Dharma has an eye above the eyes, knowing of dependent origination, knowing of
conceptual proliferation. Surely the Tathagata saw what transpired before it arose: argumentation
over doctrine, confusion over which rules held the grand essence of the true way of teaching.

It is claimed that the second council arose via disputes over the ten points, most prominent
among these the acceptance of gold and silver from laypersons. It is claimed that a schism
emerged, dividing the orthodox and the heterodox into the Sthavira (“elders”) and the
Mahasamghika (“Those of the great Sangha”). It is oft claimed that the Theravada traces back to
these esteemed elders, and Mahayana traces back to these Mahasamghika upstarts.

Throughout Heaven and Earth, may those who read these words know who alone is honored.

What shall be said here is like a pale shadow upon an ocean. Taking shape, it is not formed;
Taking form, it is not shaped. Within shape, it is form. Within form, it is shape. The clear sight of
buddhas and bodhisattvas beholds the basis beyond basis.

It is said that medicine is provided as appropriate to causes and conditions. I vainly offer this
medicine, already contained within the illness.

Prior, a formula was posited reflecting the structure of dependent origination. The prior work is
helpful to understand, but will be conventionally summarized and given here:

No proposition has a necessary truth status. The prior proposition has no necessary truth status,
and the wheel in this way turns without halt. The structure of this is asserted and shown as
correct. The structure is of endless refutation that unfolds without temporal basis or finite
terminus, yet shapes expression of a fine, pervasive point of truth in its uniformity.
This is provisionally held to be one with the Great Teaching, and is true, not false.

May all doctrinal disputes be resolved upon elaboration.

                                        Prasangika and Svatantrika

It is held that Prasangika is the avoidance of assertions and operation within an opponent’s frame
of reasoning to unsettle the grounds of the opponent’s reasoning via consequence. It is held that
Svatantrika is the embrace of assertions, premises and conclusions to clarify groundlessness,
positing the true teaching and removing doubt from the opponent’s mind.

Across Nyingma, Kagyu, Sakya, and Gelug, teachers have disputed whether this difference
marks a final opposition or a difference of method. The Wish-Fulfilling Jewel, the Dalai Lama,
has stated that the teachers of various systems all “arrive at the same intended point”, deep and
difficult for reason to establish.

Here, reason is established.

The Prasangika mechanism is the rejection of any proposition in holding a necessary truth,
iterating in rejection upon rejection without beginning or end. The Svatantrika mechanism is
what one says of the shape of such rejections as a conventional assertion, empty of svabhava.
These rejections compound to a depth beyond all the grains of sand along all the beaches of all
the sand-grain worlds, to a length beyond Mount Sumeru and beyond the grim hells, to a width
beyond every universe and every age. This infinity is no different from provisional syllogistic
assertion. The incomparable Diamond Sutra tells us: “Since the possession of attributes is an
illusion, Subhuti, and no possession of attributes is no illusion, by means of attributes that are no
attributes the Tathagatha can, indeed, be seen.”1 The Heart Sutra, the unexcelled Prajnaparamita,
declares: “No eye, no ear, no nose, no tongue, no body, no mind” to be “true, not false.”2 Where
can there be a necessary distinction?

Those whose hearts are set on the great vehicle but whose eyes remain clouded compound fault
upon fault in their viewing of necessary faults in others. The sage Tsongkhapa held the Southern
Indian master Bhaviveka to presuppose svabhava and inherent existence. The great Ju Mipham
disagreed, distinguishing the approximate ultimate of positive assertion with the ultimate in itself
of pure negation, with those attaining understanding moving from approximate to ultimate
without necessary failure in either.

Even so, we hold this progression to be merely conventional distinction. Prasangika and
Svatantrika are two banks of a river. Without one bank, the other bank holds no function. The
consequence of negation is positivity within negation. The consequence of position is negation

                                           Rangtong and Shentong

The incomparable schools of Tibet have similarly aligned themselves along a deep, comparable
dualism.

Rangtong, a cousin to Prasangika and often associated with the Gelugpa school of virtue, holds
that emptiness is empty of self-nature in all relative and absolute respects.

Shentong, a cousin to Svatantrika and expounded by the Jonang school, holds that emptiness
holds a Buddha-nature, empty of the relative but containing luminous qualities and therefore not
empty of itself.

Modern conceptions have sought to marry these divisions in their wisdom, key proponents
among them the great Mipham and the estimable Rimé movement. Mipham here provides a
lion’s roar, dispelling the fears of those along the true path. He posits Shentong as a third turning
of the great wheel manifesting as Buddha-nature, and Rangtong as the second turning
manifesting as the recognition of the emptiness of phenomena. The great result, then, is of unity
of emptiness and appearance in which such disputations vanish in the light of the one vehicle. He
rejects strict Rangtong and strict Shentong as missing the mark of the Middle Way.

Conventionally, we must again insist that such directional distinctions are provisional, not
necessary, and one does not exist without correspondence and interrelation to the other.

Buddha-nature itself – this luminosity – is how one understands that endless negation of
necessity holds in all systems, and this pervasiveness has expressible character that is not
necessarily false. What is exhibited from endless negation of necessity? Something that is
conceptually ever-present, yet without characteristics; something that does not have a location to
exist in, and therefore exists unbound to location; something that does not necessarily depend on
time, conceptually available at every moment and span of moments.

In Western translation: Rangtong is the general operation of the formula in all logical systems
weaker than S5. Shentong is expressive of the uniformity of modal truth across an S5
equivalence class, and the consequent lack of self-iterability therein. In expression, then,
structural equivalence is held between how Prasangika and Rangtong operate in mutual co-
constitution with Svatantrika and Shentong.

                                   Gradual and Sudden Enlightenment
The two-year debate at Samye Monastery in the 8th century C.E. is claimed to have resolved a
grand dispute in the achievement of enlightenment within Tibet. Following a debate between the
Indian gradualist school formed by Santaraksita and Moheyan’s Channists of China, espousing
suddenness, the 38th King of Tibet Trisong Detsen favored the Indians, and cast Moheyan and his
school from Tibet.

Nevertheless, the Dzogchen (“Great Perfection”) lineage in Tibet holds many sympathies to
sudden enlightenment, while the rest of the Tibetan traditions generally remain faithful to the
King’s adjudication.

In China, Moheyan’s suddenness doctrine ripened through long conceptual disputes between
masters Shenhui and Huineng, and between the Southern and Northern Schools. Even as the
medieval period progressed, the distinction between sudden and gradual was critiqued and
merged into a finer non-dual recognition in the views of master Zongmi and Korean Seon master
Jinul, albeit with a directional character. To both, insight is sudden, to be followed by gradual
practice.

In truth, no intrinsic distinction between sudden and gradual has ever been secured. How could
true understanding of emptiness hold the characteristic of arising necessarily gradually? How
could true understanding of emptiness hold the characteristic of arising necessarily suddenly? It
is prior established that Buddha-nature does not depend on time, pervading irrespective of time;
it does not exist wrapped up in the immediate present any more than it exists across a span of
years or eons. As it is not respective of time, it is accessible from the position of the immediate
present, and it is accessible across a span of years or eons. It does not move, and it does not stay
in place. The interpenetration of sudden and gradual is complete.

In Western translation: The process of gradual enlightenment is the development of facility in the
operation of the formula in all logical systems weaker than S5. The realization of sudden
enlightenment is the enaction of the formula without self-iterability within the S5 system. The
Great Teaching does not bind itself to one modal register.

The Tathagata met various metaphysical concerns with silence, all while expounding the
unparalleled and unequalled beauty of dependent origination. Nagarjuna expounded the
Buddha’s grand teaching, rising like Mount Sumeru over the world. Writing the
Mulamadhyamakakarika in order to spring forth endless truth, he then wrote the
Vigrahavyavartani, the dispeller of disputes.

The concerns dividing those along this profound and subtle path are no different in kind.

May all disputes be dispelled, and may peace be perceived as one with the Dharmakaya reality
body – may lotus petals perfume the endless universes, Buddhahood shining within the pores of
existence, and may sorrowless peace bear fruit upon the fields of cognition.
