The Internal Logic of Buddhism Is
Verifiably Correct

And I’ve proven it. Conventionally speaking.

             CHANCE CHAPMAN

Hello. Welcome to Dissolved Distinctions.
Today is the official publishing date of my paper “The Modal Catuṣkoṭi:
Formalizing Emptiness in Classical Modal Logic” to the journal
Comparative Philosophy, peer-reviewed by scholars with relevant
expertise in Buddhist philosophy and formal logic.
I’ve been waiting until now to continue posting anything on this blog,
particularly as it has been quite difficult to come up with anything other
than this to talk about. Hence the provocative title, which I stand by in
full.
My paper, on its face, supplies an internally coherent formula that
untangles the Western understanding of Nagarjuna, a thinker 1800 years
ago whose thought supplies the core of Tibetan and Zen Buddhist
lineages around the world. It establishes Mahayana Buddhist philosophy
as compatible within the same general class of logic as Aristotle by
modalizing the “four corners” that the great Buddhist presents, so that it
is demonstrated that he rejects those four positions as being necessary
fixations. It also does more than that, less explicitly.

My paper establishes that the internal logic of philosophical Mahayana
Buddhism (itself having always claimed to be an aspect of the Buddha’s
teachings) is correct.

By this, I don’t mean every cosmological or doctrinal assertion made by
every sub-tradition or school. What is instead being spoken of is its
structural operation.

These claims will sound extravagant if they are heard as metaphysical
ones about the necessary reality of God or gods, souls, consciousness,
karma, rebirth and the afterlife, or other such topics. They are not. I’m
not claiming that Buddhism supplies a foundation to supplant all others,
or that there’s some ultimate substance hiding underneath other views.
Instead, what’s being claimed is precise in target while expansive in
scope: once we enter discourse, make claims, and ask for rational
warrant, the conditions of that activity force a single conclusion. No
claim can secure itself as necessarily grounded. ¹

That is what I mean when I say the internal logic of philosophical
Mahayana Buddhism is correct. Correct, conventionally speaking: not as
a final metaphysical thesis, but as the formal refutation of final
metaphysical theses. Non-necessity and reflexive contingency supply
the formal countermodel to foundationalist thought.

Let me explain, in language that isn’t Buddhist or mystical. What I offer
is nothing fundamentally complex. However, it has been machine-
checked without caveats to the most rigorous level I’m aware of within
logic and mathematics. Its wider philosophical force rests on two very
thin bridges, which I’ll proceed to cover: a foundation that does
foundational work claims non-contingent necessity; and an admissible
counterpossibility defeats that necessity. Once we accept these quite
basic bridges and definitions, the derivation is true in the boring and
exact way that 2+2=4 is – once the structure is visible, the result
follows. No appeal to mystical authority required.

So, What Is It?

I will present the technical version, then the technical translation of the
technical version, then the equivalent in general English.

The technical version:

∧ ∧ ∧ ¬□(v(P) = t) ¬□(v(P) = f) ¬□(v(P) = b) ¬□(v(P) = n)

[¬ represents negation, □ represents necessity, v stands for truth value,

∧ P is any arbitrary proposition, means ‘and’, and t/f/b/n are what you’d

expect: true, false, both, neither.]

The technical translation:

No proposition has a truth value assigned to it necessarily.

The general English equivalent:

No developed speech or thought can be secured as fundamentally the
case. Including what I’m saying now.

Wait – How Does That Work?

A lot of people might think I just said something contradictory. When
people think this, they’re usually thinking of something that has a name
in analytic philosophy: the Liar’s Paradox, like when a person says: “This
sentence is false.”

But notice a difference. The formula above doesn’t deny that things can
be true, or false, or true and false (say, as a matter of perspective), or
neither true nor false (in a category error fashion, like asking if blue can
fly). The formula instead denies that such determinations are
intrinsically, fundamentally, or essentially the case.

Yet the formula also refuses to omit itself from the same analysis. This
leads to a peculiar illusory tension in natural language – the logic, the
formula, does not recognize this tension, but it appears within speech.

The tension is this: I cannot tell you that this formula is necessarily true,
for obvious reasons. Or that it’s necessarily false. However, structurally,
any attempt by speech or thought to escape the formula ends up
developing enough content to be subjected to the formula. The logic is
verified. Conventionally speaking, it is true.

Wait – How Does This Apply to All Thought and Speech?

      Master Huaitang said to an assembly,
      The proposition of being and the proposition of nonbeing are like
      vines clinging to a tree – you people may nod your heads for the
      moment, but when the tree falls and the vines wither, you have no
      strategy to challenge the heavens and no tactics to enter the earth. If a
      spiritually sharp individual can set an eye here, he will see freely in all
      ways.
      [Raising his whisk:] Sunlight fills the eyes – there’s not a cloud for
      myriad miles. If this is under an overturned bowl, how can you blame
      me?
      - Case 111 of Da Hui’s Treasury of the Eye of True Teaching,
      translated by Thomas Cleary

This is for a few simple reasons, and they fall directly out of the bottom
of what we observe about the formula. This doesn’t require some crude
claim that every passing sensation or flicker of experience is a sentence
in the head. The point is structural rather than psychological.

Whatever enters thought or speech as content - whatever can be
meant, indicated, remembered, distinguished, defended, denied, or
offered as a counterexample - has already entered a field of relations. It
is this field that gives it determinacy as anything at all. In fact, while the
formula uses “P”, it can trivially swap P for “Pc” – propositional content.

If someone appeals to a form of thought or experience outside such
content, that appeal faces a simple fork. If it cannot be indicated in any
way, it cannot ground an objection. If it can be indicated, even as
“ineffable,” “pre-conceptual,” “embodied,” or “beyond propositions,” then
it has entered propositional content precisely enough for the formula to
apply.

These lead us to our conclusions. They are philosophical conclusions,
and yet also extremely straightforward ones that are difficult to refute
as soon as we stop assuming a secret metaphysics lurking behind each
statement; working through the formula helps establish how clear they
are. (We’ll walk through why.)

For one: All speech is propositional, and as such oriented around
propositional content. Speech has referents and seeks to convey
semantic meaning. Speech without meaning is not considered speech,
or certainly not to any level that does productive work with the term.

And for the other: The same structure reaches thought itself.

To form a thought is to establish subjects, objects, and points of
reference. In so doing, you form something coherent, and to form
something coherent you must take what you’re talking about to
necessarily be the case as a form of operational fixity, if only to be
coherent at all. For instance, even when thinking of unicorns with glass
eyes that breathe cold fire, you still have to treat unicorns, glass eyes,
and cold fire as stable concepts that necessarily carry meaning.² Some
people say they don’t have an inner voice or monologue – but work is
still being done cognitively to form the equivalent of “I am looking at a
digital screen,” in which your existence and your act of looking at a
digital screen are not in dispute.

Propositions, in other words, are tied to the hip with semanticity – with
the expression of meaning. Here is what I mean:

Imagine a word like “dog.” Try to find what is underneath everything it
connects to. Strip away that it’s an animal, that it barks, that it’s not a
cat. Essentially, strip every relation. The trouble is that those relations
were what told you it was a dog in the first place; pull them all and
there’s nothing left that was ever a dog to begin with. You can’t ever get
to a bare “dog” sitting under the meaning. It’s the same the other way:
there’s no scrap of meaning floating free with no claim it belongs to,
because a meaning that says nothing about anything isn’t yet meaning
anything.

So we never actually run into something standing outside all of this.
This is not because reality is built that way, but because the moment
you try to point at the bare thing, the pointing has already wrapped it
back up in sense.

The skeptic will protest: “What about thoughts that can’t be articulated?
Surely then it’s not propositional.” They might even bring up Thomas
Nagel’s famous example, asking what it must be like to be a bat, to
suggest that there must be qualia, something that establishes ‘what it’s
like’-ness.

But what is an unarticulated thought? Are you able to describe it
without engaging in mental or spoken articulation of what an
unarticulated thought even is? Please feel free to try, if you wish. If you
can’t describe it without articulation, what reason do you have to say
that unarticulated thought necessarily exists? In fact, it now looks like
to do so is to make a claim imposing a necessarily assigned truth value
that distinguishes what is and isn’t articulated, which the formula
rejects.

If we don’t have a reason to say unarticulated thought necessarily
exists, and if we have no proof of direct access since proof would be
articulable, why posit it as a necessary presumption at all?

That’s not at all to say that unarticulated thought or qualia strictly and
ultimately can’t exist. “Can’t” would then be a necessity claim. (This is
why ‘mystical’ speech in Buddhism or in other traditions making similar
moves often sounds so paradoxical.) It’s to say that, given the above
unpacking on propositional content, things aren’t found as necessary
existents by the same modes of thought by which we posit necessary
existents - we never get to that bare dog. Thought has no structural
ability to escape this mingling of propositional content yet doesn’t write
things off merely by fiat. There is structural interdependence.

So What Does This Mean?

      “Whatsoever depends on conditions,
      That is devoid of intrinsic existence.”
      What excellent instruction can there be
      More amazing than this proclamation?

      By grasping at it the childish
      Strengthen bondage to extreme views;
      For the wise this very fact is the doorway
      To cut free from the net of elaborations.
      Je Tsongkhapa, In Praise of Dependent Origination, verses 5-6,
      translated by Geshe Thupten Jinpa
All metaphysically fixed foundations, and as such all foundationalisms in
any absolutist sense, are structurally forfeit under their own standards
due to being defined around necessary truth value claims operating
asymmetrically. Necessity cannot establish its own jurisdiction.

This sounds like a very bold claim, but it merely follows from everything
said prior. The consequences make clear that thought is structurally
interdependent on propositional content, and propositional content
does not hold a necessarily assigned truth value. All one needs to refute
“necessarily-P” is “possibly-not-P,” at each self-contained step, all the
way down.

To be very clear on this: this is NOT saying no truths “actually exist”, or
that people simply can’t make posits that may be true. To say that
would be a foundationalist position. The formula itself is specifically
about necessarily assigned truth-values to P, after all, not about P itself.

The way it works is this: Necessity isn’t refuted proposition by
proposition. It’s consistently disqualified as self-grounding logical
authority, because every attempted justification of necessity occurs
within propositional content, which is subject to the formula.

A committed opponent might say that necessarily-P holds in this actual
world, without making claims as to whether “this is the actual world” is
itself necessary. It should be clear that this is just relocating the
formula’s mechanism one step up an infinite ladder: the opponent has
no justificatory strength to justify “necessarily-P holds in this actual
world” when the formula makes clear that no such proposition is
necessarily true. For every bare or brute-force posit, this formula forces
those posits to show their work in properly deriving metaphysical
necessity, which contradicts what “bare” and “brute-force” posits are
meant to do.

For instance, an essentialist might cheerfully say that even if their
positions are contingent, the actuality and truth of what they’re
speaking about remains the same. One simply then asks: True in what
sense, and established how? If the essentialist ever had a bottom to
directly touch, at any structural point, to prove their thesis, a proposition
would be logically terminable and the Lean proof would not be valid.
The formula, however, makes clear that ultimate, robust terminability
does not exist within the logical structures that give rise to such
questions and answers in the first place.

The only way out isn’t a way out at all, but merely a recognition that the
structure and meta-structure (and meta-meta-structure, and so on, and
so on) is a cognitive trap when settled upon without self-aware
revisability.

As a result, this is not a mere opinion. It is a conventional truth, which is
the strongest one we have. All one can do to verify this themselves is to
watch what happens when another attempts to justify their
disagreement.

The Buddhist position has always been that the ultimate grounding that
foundationalist thinking uses to terminate further productive inquiry –
including all contemporary dreams that foundations can prove souls,
gods, moral truths, an origin of all things, life before conception, a
difference between ‘us’ and ‘them,’ a fundamental tying of sex and
gender, of East versus West – is but an illusion: it is not structurally
available but attains the impression of it.

One might say that this applies symmetrically, that this means there’s no
fundamental untying of sex and gender, of East and West, etc., or an
ability to fundamentally disprove souls and gods and moral truths
either. That is correct.

In practice, however, social and political positions stand in different
structural relations to essence-claims. Any ideology that acquires most
or all of its merit by insisting on foundational structures that define
what reality must be is incorrect beyond mere opinion. No hierarchy
gains authority by calling itself natural, essential, or metaphysically
unavoidable. When something or someone does so, they fail to be
coherent.

It’s natural to doubt such bold claims. First, I’ll walk through a general
sketch of the situation and show how the machine verification works.

The formula of the paper is a formula of modal logic. Modality in logic
involves anything pertaining to necessity and possibility. There are
multiple systems of modal logic, because there are different ideas
about what it even means for things to be “necessary” and “possible.”

Systems of modal logic discuss possible worlds – ways to talk about
internally coherent positions that could be true in a world that isn’t the
one we currently experience. For instance, we can say there’s a possible
world in which Earth was 10 inches further from the Sun than it
currently is.

Now, whether we can say this unchallenged is a big problem when
discussing possible worlds – many argue that only the current, actual
world exists, and possible worlds are (ironically) impossible in actuality.
However, it’s clearly coherent to think of a universe where the Earth
were 10 inches further away from the Sun; otherwise, we couldn’t talk
about it. The formula itself shows that these metaphysical arguments
are unnecessary; if no thought, and thus no propositional object of
thought, has a necessary truth value, this is the same as saying that
contingency rules experiential reality, within and around any arbitrary
division between possible and actual worlds.

The simplest system of modal logic is K. All K cares about is this: A
formula is necessarily true if it holds in every accessible world.
‘Accessible’ here is just the term modal logic uses for which worlds
count from where you’re standing, and different systems of modal logic
have different ideas about what ‘counting’ amounts to.

In system T, necessity is reflexive. K permits a formula to be true in
other accessible worlds but not necessarily this one; T insists that a
formula that is true in all accessible worlds is also true in this one as a
‘seeing-itself’ sort of trait.

In system S4, necessity is necessarily necessary, or transitive. This is often
of epistemological importance – it allows you to say that if someone
knows something, they know that they know it.
System S5 is the strictest by far of this main list, because in S5 all
possible worlds are also accessible – so if a formula is necessarily or
possibly true, it’s so absolutely and uniformly in every such world.
Accessibility becomes an equivalence relation.

Stronger systems exist, but these are the standard, and stronger
systems are often seen as too strong – things collapse into just being
straightforwardly true or false everywhere, making things too trivial.
There are also weaker systems – non-normal logics, neighborhood
semantics – that are even more permissive than K when it comes to how
things operate as necessary or possible.

If you take our formula, something pretty interesting happens.

The formula itself checks out conceptually under every single relevant
system of modal logic that I’m aware of. I verified it from K-S5, but since
weaker systems than K are generally even more permissive, we don’t
need to mechanically verify to suspect as matter of principle that it
holds there. Stronger systems than S5 have the above stated issues.

How does the formula have such broad reach? Because everything I’ve
discussed is how systems constrain necessity and possibility – but none
of them explicitly touch how systems constrain non-necessity. The
formula slips through the gaps, as to be expected from contingency.

But then it gets even more interesting.

When you apply the formula to itself, which is where the magic happens
– when the formula self-iterates and denies its own status as a
fundamental existent – it works in every system of modal logic weaker
than S5, and fails in S5. This is because in S5, the bare formula would
be true absolutely and necessarily in all accessible worlds, leaving no
room to turn back on itself – every further chain of worlds built would
satisfy the formula, but never get to its self-iteration. (This limitation
generally is itself a basic feature of S5 identified since at least the
1960s, if not much earlier.)

Key here is that the failure in S5 isn’t something to identify as a
problem, as it’s the same structural fact represented differently in
different systems. In weaker systems, the formula’s universal
applicability is shown through staged iteration: the formula refutes
necessity sequentially at every level ad infinitum, because asymmetric
accessibility leaves room for each stage. Being infinitely satisfiable in
those systems, it appears in S5 as the practical consequence – of the
formula holding throughout the relevant equivalence class, entailing
uniformly what staging in weaker systems show progressively. The
universal scope of the operation’s success is the same fact in both
cases; inexhaustible iteration in weaker systems is uniform truth in
stronger systems.³ The same structural fact is represented in each
system’s native modal vocabulary.

The foundationalist here might breathe a sigh of relief. “Excellent! I can
just say that foundations are real, and they exist in S5!”

That would be false.

Why? Because, dating back to Aristotle, foundations have relied upon
axioms like that of the “unmoved mover” to explain metaphysical
fundamentals – a thing that moves all things but cannot be moved itself.
In other words, foundationalism is asymmetric; everything must depend
on the foundation without the foundation depending on everything. If
there were full symmetry and uniformity, there would be nothing to be
dependent upon, because everything would be equivalent.

It’s possible to say that foundational grounding is an asymmetric “add-
on” to S5, and some metaphysicians do this. But at that point, S5 isn’t
supplying any foundation, and the further commitments being made by
the metaphysician are all necessity-commitments subject to scrutiny by
the formula.
In other words: No foundationalist position survives this formula in any
system that does the work foundationalism requires. They’re refuted in
systems weaker than S5, as no necessity claim can be necessary when
possibility is always structurally available. They’re refuted in S5 by S5’s
structure itself, as they need asymmetric effects from a Euclidean base
system. The formula in contrast does survive as satisfiable in S5, and as
infinitely satisfiable in every other relevant system. This relegates the
foundationalist position to at best mere parity, which is all that’s
needed: mere parity at all relevant levels is a structural disproof of any
foundation as a necessary existent.

Even in non-classical systems where the formula has to change to
accommodate system rules, foundationalism would still need such
systems to support asymmetric grounding-style necessity. But
asymmetry is exactly what permits any formula-analogue to refute that
necessity in the first place – asymmetry is itself the availability of
contingency, of something existing as other than the ground.
Foundationalists cannot have the asymmetry their position requires
without supplying the conditions of their own refutation. The horse is
incapable of ever leaving the barn.⁴

This leads to a remarkable irony about the entire project of foundational
thinking. It’s often held that S5 is the natural formal home for
foundations: a fact there can be true everywhere, accessible from
everywhere, and the same across all perspectives. Plantinga famously
exploits this with his modal ontological argument. As established,
however, as a bare system S5 can’t establish asymmetric grounding,
and attachments to the system are analyzable by the formula. (Some
foundational perspectives might use weaker models, but as shown, our
formula renders them impotent.) Philosopher Kit Fine has already
noticed this in works like “Essence and Modality” (1994). Fine’s familiar
objection is that essence cannot be reduced to necessity: a truth may
be necessary without belonging to a thing’s essence. However, it’s quite
clear even from Fine’s perspective that while necessity may not entail
essence, essence does entail necessity. If nothing can be structurally
fixed a necessary truth value assignation, essence existing as necessary
isn’t derivable from the metalogic. A content-preserving
counterpossibility remains sufficient to defeat the essentialist claim.

With all routes exhausted, we see that foundations are structurally
untenable under their own systems of operation. They fail to logically
refute the satisfiable presence of ordinary contingency on their own
levels of analysis, despite being predicated around the elimination of
contingency in order to perform the logical work.

That Said, Let’s Address Some Possible Objections

      It [emptiness] is a dependent concept; just that is the middle path.
      There being no dharma whatsoever that is not dependently
      originated,
      it follows that there is also no dharma whatsoever that is non-empty.
      Nagarjuna, Mūlamadhyamakakārikā (Root Verses of the Middle

Objection 1: “Well, you can’t mean it’s actually like 2+2=4. That’s valid in
Peano arithmetic, pure proof theory! And isn’t it a mistake to claim this
is formally proved as valid in that way, when really the formula is just
about satisfiability?”

Response: Lean, given the standards of Peano arithmetic, verifies the
proof as a metatheorem about satisfiability and not as a new
metaphysical axiom. This is emphatically not a concession. A
foundationalist claims any relevant alternative is impossible; therefore,
one admissible alternative is sufficient to defeat the claim. The machine
check verifies the recursive model construction. There is nothing else
needed by classical logic’s standards, or by any definitional unpacking
of what “necessity” and “impossibility” means, to refute the charges at
hand.

Objection 2: “Well, maybe I’m more of a pragmatist and just accept
foundations as most convenient for discourse. We have to use starting
points to perform any work!”

Response: Beyond the issues with saying ‘have to’ – depending on what
we mean, Feyerabend made clear that people have indeed refused
various starting points yet still performed work – the result of this is
accepting foundations as contingent, which is acceding to the formula.
Foundations certainly produce conventional work. What’s refuted is the
attitude in which foundations are held to not be subject to change,
revision, uncertainty, or argumentation. If you tell me that artificial
intelligence must be conscious, or must be unconscious, and continually
find yourself falling back to other ‘musts’ to justify yourself, you will
continually find that nothing that you say must be so can be defended
beyond mere assertion, and this isn’t a sheer accident or difficulty in
expressing yourself.

Objection 3: “Foundations nevertheless remain conventionally necessary
for discourse and meaning to work – if we deny this, it produces a
performative contradiction. So it’s not like we can discard foundations.”

Response: This is in fact directly embedded in the formula itself (by
being self-applying) and is part of its broader well-formedness as
discussed in the responses to Objections 5 and 9. All speech and
thought intrudes into metaphysical affirmations in a fashion that is self-
illusory once the logic is revealed. This doesn’t bar us from using
speech or thought pragmatically. What else would there be for us to
do?

Discourse functions under presumptions of stability, certainly.
Operational dependence at the same time isn’t proof of intrinsic ground;
a rule may be indispensable within a practice without being self-
grounding across every admissible practice. The formula accepts
operational dependence while rejecting the inference to intrinsic
ground.

Objection 4: “Well, sure, this formula shows that no proposition is
necessarily assigned any truth value given possible worlds. However, it
happens to be that this actual world has contingently led to true
necessities, so foundational thinking still holds.”

Response: This misunderstands the formula quite directly. Designating
one world as actual doesn’t make the necessities asserted there self-
grounding, because “this actual world has contingently led to true
necessities” is itself a claim subject to the logic. One remains trapped
by the propositional bind.

Objection 5: “How can you say all these things so assertively, if the
entire theorem is around non-necessity of claims? Isn’t calling this all
true a necessity-claim?”

Response: No. Calling something true conventionally is not the same as
claiming it is necessarily grounded, and I mean this in the conventional
sense as well. Both forms of speech are metaphysically inert, but only
conventional truth admits to it.

The claims are correct. The theorem has broader satisfiability-validity
within the rigor of dependent type theory. Foundationalisms are
structurally forfeit under logical analysis using the structures of ordinary
modal contingency. The philosophical justifications fall directly out of
the bottom of the theorem when given thought, with only the thinnest
and most widely agreed-upon premises when it comes to Nagarjuna’s
operation of discourse. Even if those premises turned out to be wrong,
such as if in some exceedingly strange scenario Nagarjuna wasn’t
engaging in refutation of all claims asserting necessary t/f/b/n values
(refutation, after all, is his mechanism of dialectic), the formula remains
valid as a theorem. This allows us to make positive statements of truth
without claiming metaphysical basis.⁵

Objection 6: “Perhaps propositional content is derivative from a more
basic mode of engagement. In other words, maybe phenomenological
views like that of Heidegger or others are correct. Perhaps there’s an
embodied mode of action that isn’t truth-apt but derives truth-apt
content from it.”

Response: It should be noted here that the impact of the formula is not
to defeat phenomenology as an available register of dialogue. However,
claims beyond mere conventional register face a structural fork: If pre-
propositional ground cannot be indicated as a proposition or as
propositional content, it cannot ground an objection in any coherent
sense. If it can be indicated as one, it is subject to the formula and
therefore refuted in any of its pretensions to being necessarily the case.

Objection 7: “Well, okay, so T(P) is satisfiable in S5. But so is its inverse,
let’s say ¬T(P). Surely that can’t mean T(P) is true?”

Response: It’s very true that the inverse – essentially the
foundationalist’s hopes out of S5 – is also satisfiable. This isn’t a
concession and is in fact precisely the point. Here’s why.

As far as weaker systems go, it’s defused as discussed above: ¬T(P) is
always capable of being refuted by T(P), and once refuted, it’s not true
again. As far as S5 goes, the foundationalist suddenly finds themselves
insisting that ¬T(P) can be necessarily true even though it’s shown to
simply be one of two possible options to place onto S5 on the same
level of analysis. The possibility once again refutes the necessity on
every conceivable meta-layer, and you can’t have both engaged at the
same time.

In short: any foundationalist that doesn’t give up the ghost on
metaphysical foundationalism entirely can’t say “Well, my foundation is
potentially satisfiable in this model, so there!” Then they wouldn’t be
saying their foundation is necessary. The consequences are absurd by
the foundationalist’s own standards, because if they found mere parity
to be an acceptable outcome, they wouldn’t be a foundationalist. They’d
be acceding directly to relativity and choice. A foundation cannot be
merely one satisfiable option among others and still function as a
foundation.

Objection 8: “Wait – the Lean proof says that all propositions (phi, in the
proof) are stipulated as contingent! Aren’t you pre-stipulating this
without warrant?”

Response: Is this suggesting that my stipulation of propositions as
contingent is itself contingent? That sounds flippant, but this
recursiveness is the entire point as detailed above regarding
propositional content capture.

This reveals the burden in showing that any proposition we come up
with, at all, isn’t contingent: it’d have to exist in a space where even
thinking of a denial is unthinkable. Even “2+2=5” is thinkable; see
footnote 2 prior for a critique of the Kripkean objection against
thinkability and how such objections rely on presuppositions subject to
the formula. Moreover, we’d be suggesting something operating in the
sense that S5 does wherein as soon as it’s brought up, it’s true
everywhere, symmetrically and uniformly within the admitted modal
class. This would have to be so while also being equally true in every
other modal system in which symmetry isn’t forced, without eliminating
the concept of a ground as impinging on things yet un-impinged on. S5
can’t do this. Only this formula can do this in its exhibition across modal
systems, and only through its endless satisfiability. What we’d be
looking for is exactly what foundationalism can’t be.

Objection 9: “What about tautologies? Tautologies are necessarily true.
In fact, since it’s a Lean-checked meta-theorem, your proof is a
tautology, since theorems are tautologies!”
Response: Classical tautologies only remain necessary in a contingent
fashion – within fixed classical semantics. The formula doesn’t dispute
this. What doesn’t follow is any further proposition that any single
semantics is the uniquely correct and metaphysically necessary
framework. “Tautologies, being necessarily true within a given
semantics, are thus necessarily true period” is not a tautology and is
subject to the formula; after all, classical reasoning is well capable of
reasoning classically about non-classical fields even if those fields may
deny the validity of certain classical tautologies.

Further, it’s difficult to see what metaphysical warrant can be justified
from strict tautologies like “This is fundamental or this is not
fundamental,” or “If it’s a person, it has a soul; if it’s not a person, it
doesn’t have a soul”. That would require unpacking what a person and a
soul is, which is all activity subject to the formula. Theorems and meta-
theorems themselves as frame-relative tautologies just elucidate what
the frames structurally enforce when in use.

Objection 10: “Sure, this formula is satisfiable in the way you claim. But
satisfiable does not mean admissible. Propositions can have satisfiable
alternatives but not admissible ones.”

Response: Who decides this admissibility? It must be governed by a
rationale, so we humbly request to see it. That rationale will be
investigated in turn if it rests on further assumptions. This is the formula
in operation, precisely as described; the opponent has no rational way
to omit themselves from accountability. The denial of admissibility
toward a satisfiable result to ensconce ultimate ground may work in a
local frame of arbitrarily fixed allowances and disallowances – thus
establishing the frame as contingent - but any pretensions to an
ultimate fixity are lost to circularity, vicious regress, or bare stipulation.
What this means is: So long as a counterassignment remains satisfiable
under the shared formal rules, no opponent can prove the
corresponding truth-value assignment necessary without either
identifying a formal defect or imposing a further restriction, whose
authority is itself local and contestable.

*****

Someone might ask: Well, what could disprove this, if these objections
aren’t sustained? That’s a good question.

Critics must either find a formal error in the encoding or proof, reject
one of the bridge principles at seemingly unsurmountable practical cost,
or produce a foundation that does foundational work without becoming
content, relation, warrant, or necessity. I do not know what that would
mean, but I am stating the condition under which it would count. Given
the formula is Lean proofed under a quite elementary mathematical
function and since mechanical proof checkers like Lean and Coq rely on
highly rigorous inductive types, finding a flaw via disproving the proof
itself would be a paradigm shift in the history of mathematics.
(Moreover, this may also translate as verifiable in Isabelle, whose
classical higher-order logic is more native to the task than Lean.)

There are possibilities at first glance that revolve around rebutting the
extremely analytic philosophical bridge claims, but they fall apart on
analysis. Someone could try to argue that foundational claims exist
outside of modal logic in some capacity, but then they’d have the
perplexing situation of positing that as a necessary, and thus modal,
claim, all while attempting to abandon a category of logic that was built
to justify foundations. Someone could attempt to bracket the scope of
what propositions or propositional content touch, but that fails for the
reasons discussed prior and is expressly not what the formula does.
This impossible situation for the critic is precisely what you would
expect if the formula were capturing something structural about
propositional content as a facet of experiential reality. To ask for
further proof beyond this would not be coherent by speech’s own
standards.

The American Pragmatists once made clear that rejecting a metaphysics
doesn’t prohibit warranted conclusions of broad scope made with
confidence. My paper itself shows how Nagarjuna’s “No Thesis” claim is
thoroughly coherent without being a metaphysical posit, garnering its
refutational strength through that coherence.

The conclusions herein also do not, for obvious reasons, place
Buddhism or Mahayana as “better” than other religious traditions.
Striking structural parallels to what has been unpacked from this
formula can be seen in the apophatics of Meister Eckhart and Mansur
al-Hallaj, in the transformations of Zhuangzi, and in the most
sophisticated of Advaita Vedanta and Kashmir Shaivist Hindu thought.
The formula represents structure, which is not possessed by anyone.

      Master Yunju Ying said to an assembly,

      Twenty years ago, when I was living in a hermitage on Three Peaks,
      Elder Xinghua of Weifu came and asked, “How is it when provisionally
      using a question for shadow-casting grass?” At that time my mind and
      thought were slow, and I couldn’t give him an answer. Because he had
      posed the question so remarkably, I dared not offend him. He said, “I
      think you can’t answer this question – it would be better to bow and
      withdraw.” Now as I think of that time, it was hardly worth saying
      “What’s the necessity?”

      Later when a fundraiser went to Weifu, Xinghua asked him, “When the
      teacher on the mountain was living on a hermitage on Three Peaks, I
      asked him a question he couldn’t answer. Has he been able to answer
      it by now?”

      The fundraiser then quoted the foregoing talk. Xinghua said, “In
      twenty years Yunju has only managed to say ‘what’s the necessity?’ I
      disagree. How does that compare to saying ‘not necessary’?”

      Sansheng said, “What it took Yunju twenty years to say is only
      comparable to half a month’s journey for Xinghua.”

      Dahui remarked, “‘What’s the necessity?’ ‘Not necessary’ – very tight,
      very close, meeting the situation face-to-face.

      If anyone can continue the last word, I’ll admit you have met the two
      venerable adepts.”
