# Bonus: the ideas behind the song

Writing by Chance Chapman (Rollofthedice) on Madhyamaka Buddhist philosophy: the logic of emptiness that the song sings and the film stages.
Each is included as a PDF and as plain text (`text/`) so it can be read by screen readers and language models.

| file | what it is |
|---|---|
| `The_Modal_Catuskoti_Formalizing_Emptiness_in_Classical_Modal_Logic.pdf` | The peer-reviewed paper: *Comparative Philosophy* 17.2 (2026): 4–17, open access, https://doi.org/10.31979/2151-6014(2026).170205. A modal extension of Belnap–Dunn four-valued semantics that formalises Nāgārjuna's catuṣkoṭi (tetralemma) while keeping modus tollens: ¬□(v(P) = t) ∧ ¬□(v(P) = f) ∧ ¬□(v(P) = b) ∧ ¬□(v(P) = n). No proposition has a necessary truth status, including this one. |
| `Formalizing_Buddhist_Emptiness_Without_Losing_Modus_Tollens.pdf` | A plain-language introduction to the paper, by the author, published on Marcus Arvan's blog (18 August 2026). |
| `The_Internal_Logic_of_Buddhism_Is_Verifiably_Correct.pdf` | An essay on the paper's meaning for Buddhist thought, from the author's Substack *Dissolved Distinctions* (3 August 2026): https://rollofthedice2.substack.com/p/the-internal-logic-of-buddhism-is |
| `Severing_the_Severances.pdf` | A devotional and philosophical text: it opens in homage, recalls the early Buddhist councils and schisms, and restates the formula's structure of endless refutation as teaching. |

The bridge of the song is the tetralemma in verse: *"Neither existent nor non-existent, nor both, nor neither … Master Nāgārjuna knew: all this is
empty. Your soul too is dependently arisen. But we know this. Therefore we dance."*

The published paper keeps its journal's open-access terms; the other texts are released with the rest of this repository.
